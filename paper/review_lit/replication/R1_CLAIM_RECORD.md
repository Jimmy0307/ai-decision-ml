# R1 step 0: record of the original study's measures and claims

**Recorded:** 2026-11-19, before any data file was downloaded (preregistration R1, step 0).

## Access

- **Full text not accessed.** The article is paywalled. The open copies that were tried either failed or were not the article:
  - the author's site (TLS failure);
  - the SSRN abstract page (HTTP 403);
  - one university repository copy (HTTP 403).
- **What the record rests on:**
  - the published abstract (RePEc/IDEAS, EconBiz);
  - the first author's blog post announcing the article;
  - secondary literature that summarises the article.
- Anything taken only from secondary sources is marked **[secondary]**.

## Record

**Setting:**
- The Norwegian law requiring at least 40% of each sex on the boards of public limited companies, with full compliance due by 2008 (January 2008 per the author's blog).
- The article treats the law as an external shock.

**Data and period:** board interlock data on Norwegian public limited companies, May 2002 to August 2009. The analysis snapshot is August 2009 (author's blog). The public dataset was later extended to August 2011.

**Questions:** gender bias, the emergence and sex of prominent directors, and directors' social capital.

**Measures:**
- The abstract speaks of several proxies of influence and of social capital without naming them.
- **[secondary]** Later studies identify the proxies as **betweenness centrality** (in the director network) and the **number of directorships**.
  - One later study reads high betweenness as connecting other directors and influencing flows of knowledge or information, a "knowledge broker" reading.
  - Another reports that women had higher **closeness**.
- **[secondary]** One later paper summarises the finding as women having greater betweenness than men while seldom chairing boards.
- No source consulted mentions Burt constraint or effective size.

**Main claim:** the law raised women's representation, but it also produced a small elite of women directors who rank at the top on several proxies of influence (the "golden skirts").

## Consequence for R1 (rule of §1, step 0)

- **What the article claims:** influence or prominence read from betweenness and the number of directorships.
- **Where the brokerage reading comes from:** the explicit "broker" reading of betweenness appears in the citing literature; it could not be confirmed in the article itself.
- **So the R1 statistics map as follows:**
  - S1 (women minus men, mean betweenness) and S2 (median betweenness of multi-seat directors) test the article's betweenness-based prominence claim, and the brokerage reading that the citing literature gives it.
  - S3 and S4 (constraint) test the generic structural-hole reading of these data. They are **not** claims of the article, and they will be reported as such.
